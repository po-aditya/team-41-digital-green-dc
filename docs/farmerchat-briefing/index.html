Vision document summary

The Vision document describes a hackathon MVP for a MEL (Monitoring, Evaluation and Learning) Agent for FarmerChat. FarmerChat is Digital Green's AI advisory assistant for small-scale farmers. The agent reads English insights that FarmerChat's insight extractor already stores for each farmer. It then generates short, tap-based follow-up surveys about the specific advice that farmer received. Each survey gets a send window from the crop calendar and the advice's action window, and is handed to a scheduler and broadcaster. The scope is Bihar, India, rabi season (November to March), with synthetic data, a simulated app and simulated delivery. Quality and timeliness matter more than scale. Quality must be shown through evaluation against a Digital Green golden dataset, with at least one improvement iteration. The agent must check its own output before scheduling, and must generate nothing when an insight does not support a meaningful question. Safety is a standing requirement: no personal information requested, no new or contradictory agronomic advice, and negative answers routed for follow-up. RCTs (Randomized Controlled Trials) remain the verification method.

Source facts and open questions
  • [Executive Summary] Goal: a MEL Agent that reads FarmerChat insights and generates short, personalized follow-up surveys timed to the crop calendar, handed to a scheduler and broadcaster. Proven by evaluations against a Digital Green golden dataset, for Bihar farmers, November to March. Digital Green intends a pilot with around 10,000 farmers if quality holds.
  • [FarmerChat Today] FarmerChat serves around 2.2 million farmers by text, voice or photo. It runs on AWS with memory and around 20 MCP servers, including AgMCP. The query path is alignment agent, main agent, then insight extractor. Resolution types: direct action, indirect action, wait and watch. Insights are stored in English. Agents return JSON that the app renders as UI.
  • [The Background Agent Vision] NightSync, SeasonPlanner and FarmWatch are future phases. The MEL Agent is the hackathon focus.
  • [Problem Statement] Field surveys and RCTs are expensive and do not scale. Earlier digital and voice surveys were broadcast and generic. Irrelevant questions cost farmer trust.
  • [Business Drivers] Drivers: adoption data at scale, context already exists, timeliness, trust (quality over reach). RCTs stay as the verifier. The source claims a working system would be a first for the development sector.
  • [Target Users and Stakeholders] Users: small-scale farmers in Bihar (mobile app, voice in future), the Digital Green MEL team, the Digital Green product and AI team, and an external evaluation agency that needs consistent, auditable data collection.
  • [Business Constraints] Quality and timeliness, not infrastructure. Do not bombard farmers. Synthetic data only (fully synthetic, not anonymized). Contract first: REST API plus JSON survey object. All outputs open source.
  • [Core Concepts] Defines insight, contextual survey, survey item (one tap question with fixed options), survey schedule (send window), broadcaster, agent-to-UI JSON, and golden dataset. FarmerChat is not available at the hackathon.
  • [Design Principles] One farmer, one context. Short and tappable. Ask at the right time. Quality before volume (a missed survey beats an irrelevant one). Evaluate before scheduling (schema, relevance, language). Contract over framework. Language is a BCP 47 parameter (for example hi-IN, en-IN).
  • [Safety Tenet] Never collect personal information through a survey. Do not give new agronomic advice or contradict the advice received. Answers suggesting the advice failed or the crop was damaged should be routed back for follow-up, not only recorded.
  • [MVP Success Criteria] Ingest about 5,000 to 10,000 synthetic insights for 200 to 500 farmers. Generate valid JSON per eligible insight that references the specific advice and resolution. Send window consistent with crop calendar and action window. Score against the golden dataset for relevance, timeliness and format. Measure generation time per survey and per dataset. Scheduler orders by send window with a per-farmer frequency cap. Broadcaster stub delivers to a simulated tap app. Failed surveys are not scheduled. English end to end; Hindi via hi-IN.
  • [Features In Scope (MVP)] Must Have: insight ingestion, survey generation agent, survey JSON contract, self-evaluation step (schema plus relevance), evaluation harness (repeated runs), survey scheduler. Should Have: broadcaster stub and simulated app, Hindi surveys, latency and cost telemetry. Exploratory: response write-back, negative-outcome follow-up, alternative channels.
  • [Features Explicitly Out of Scope (MVP)] Out of scope: other background agents, production integration, real delivery, AgMCP and live external data, other languages, other regions, replacing RCTs, delivery at scale.
  • [MVP User Journeys] Journey 1: Hindi aphid question on mustard, direct action within two days if no rain; a three-item Hindi survey; send window five to seven days after the advice, after a weekly survey check. Journey 2: the MEL team runs the full dataset, scores, adjusts and reruns. Journey 3 (stretch): a negative answer is written back and a follow-up case is created.
  • [MVP Definition of Done] All Must Have features implemented and tested. Full dataset run. Golden-dataset scores with at least one improvement iteration. Time per survey and per batch reported. Invalid or low-scoring surveys verified not scheduled. Schema validation. Code, IaC, evaluation harness and README published open source.
  • [Key Risks] Risks: scope may be too small for 2 to 2.5 days; late data (mitigation: agree schema, generate a small sample); Hindi quality hard to judge; JSON mismatch with the Digital Green format (agree schema before the event); farmer overload (frequency cap).

  • [Open Questions] How should the send window be derived when advice depends on weather?
  • [Open Questions] Which automatic checks (schema, rubric, LLM-as-judge) best predict golden-dataset and MEL reviewer acceptance?

Technical document summary

The Technical document sets the build environment. Quality, proven with evaluations, is the main goal; infrastructure and scale are secondary. FarmerChat, Digital Green AWS accounts and real data are not available. Teams use AWS Workshop Studio accounts with Python 3.12 or later. Google ADK is preferred, but Strands Agents or another framework is acceptable if the REST plus JSON contract is met. Amazon Bedrock is required, with the model kept behind an abstraction. Model tiering is suggested. Hosting may be AgentCore Runtime or containers (production is moving to Amazon EKS). If a database is used, use RDS for PostgreSQL or Aurora PostgreSQL. Evaluation is central: hill-climb against the golden dataset. Dimensions are relevance, timeliness, format, safety and speed. Deterministic checks cover format and timing, and a rubric-grounded LLM-as-judge covers relevance. The document gives draft insight, survey and response JSON contracts, a shared simulated clock with manual triggers, and a rule to deliver only to the simulated app.

Claude option A1

Evaluation-first generation core with hill-climb run log

Build the smallest generator that emits contract-valid surveys from insights, and score it against the golden dataset across repeated runs. Evaluation is treated as the mandatory baseline, not as a differentiator.

Scope from the sources. Source fact: ingestion, generation, schema, and evaluation harness are Must Have. Scope: load insights; generate a survey per insight through Bedrock Converse; validate with Pydantic; score format and timing deterministically; score relevance with an LLM-as-judge; save each run with its prompt version.

Minimum demo proposed by Claude. Two scored runs on the same golden subset, with different prompt versions, side by side, plus one valid survey JSON shown next to its source insight.

AWS mapping, workflow, source support, and limits

AWS mapping

  • Amazon Bedrock (Runtime, Converse API): Survey generation and LLM-as-judge Mandatory per Technical. Confirm enabled models and region with event staff.
  • Amazon S3: Store run manifests, generated surveys, and score files Optional. Confirm availability in the Workshop Studio account. Local files are an acceptable fallback.

Inputs

  • Synthetic insights dataset
  • Golden dataset (format to confirm)
  • Crop calendar (source to confirm)
  • Draft survey schema
  • MEL rubric, if one exists

Implementation workflow

  1. Load insights and the golden dataset; join on an agreed key.
  2. Generate a survey JSON per insight via the model abstraction.
  3. Validate against the Pydantic schema; record failures.
  4. Run deterministic format and timing checks.
  5. Run a rubric-grounded LLM-as-judge for relevance.
  6. Write the run manifest; change the prompt; rerun; compare runs.

Source support

  • Vision: MVP Success Criteria (scored against golden dataset for relevance, timeliness, format)
  • Vision: MVP Definition of Done (at least one improvement iteration shown)
  • Vision: Features In Scope (MVP) (survey generation agent, survey JSON contract, evaluation harness: Must Have)
  • Technical: Evaluation and Quality Measurement (hill-climb; deterministic checks plus LLM-as-judge)
  • Technical: Core Data Contracts (draft, to align with Digital Green's insight and agent-to-UI formats)
  • Technical: Models and AI Services (Bedrock required; model behind an abstraction)

Proposed extension. Proposed: a run manifest that records prompt version, model ID, and per-dimension scores, plus a per-run comparison table so the hill-climb trend is visible. The model ID sits behind a thin provider interface.

Dependencies

  • Golden dataset arrival and format
  • Bedrock model access
  • Agreed join key between golden and generated surveys
  • Rubric decision for the judge

Risks

  • An LLM-as-judge may not match MEL reviewer judgement (source Open Question).
  • Without a rubric, relevance scores are hard to defend.
  • The improvement shown may come from noise unless the run setup is held constant.
  • Hindi cannot be scored automatically against the English baseline.

Completion check. At least two runs are recorded with per-dimension scores against the golden dataset, and every scored survey passes or is flagged by schema validation.

Claude’s proposed cuts. Run on a golden subset first. Defer full-dataset runs, S3, and dashboards. Keep the judge prompt simple and documented.

Claude option A2

Eligibility gate and tiered models (abstain when no meaningful question)

Make 'quality before volume' explicit: decide per insight whether a survey is warranted, and log the abstain reason. Explore the suggested small-model and large-model tiering.

Scope from the sources. Source fact: eligibility is implied but not defined. Scope: a gate step before generation that returns eligible or not eligible with a reason code; optional grouping of insights per farmer; token and time logging per step.

Minimum demo proposed by Claude. A small batch where some insights are skipped with visible reason codes, the rest produce surveys, and per-step tokens and time are shown.

AWS mapping, workflow, source support, and limits

AWS mapping

  • Amazon Bedrock (Converse API): Smaller model for eligibility, larger model for generation Mandatory per Technical. Which tier models are enabled must be confirmed.
  • Amazon CloudWatch: Optional logs and metrics for tokens and latency per step Optional. Confirm account permissions. Local logs are an acceptable fallback.

Inputs

  • Synthetic insights
  • Golden dataset (to infer expected eligibility, if possible)
  • Draft insight schema (resolution_type, action_window_days, topic)

Implementation workflow

  1. Apply cheap deterministic pre-filters on the fields present.
  2. Call the small model for an eligibility decision plus a reason code.
  3. Send eligible insights to the generation model.
  4. Log tokens, estimated cost, and time per step.
  5. Report abstain counts by reason and compare with the golden data.

Source support

  • Vision: Design Principles (Quality before volume: generate nothing if the insight does not support a meaningful question)
  • Vision: Open Questions (Which insights deserve a survey at all; should several insights be combined?)
  • Vision: MVP Success Criteria ('For each eligible insight')
  • Technical: Models and AI Services (tiering: smaller model for eligibility and checks, larger for generation)
  • Technical: Evaluation and Quality Measurement (Speed: time per survey and for the full dataset)

Proposed extension. Proposed: a reason-code list (for example, a missing advice summary, or the outcome not observable in the season) as a reviewable artifact for the MEL team. Proposed: compare gate decisions with golden dataset coverage, if the golden set implies which insights get surveys.

Dependencies

  • Two suitable Bedrock models enabled, or one model used for both tiers
  • Golden dataset signals about which insights should be surveyed
  • Agreement on what 'eligible' means

Risks

  • Over-abstaining hides coverage problems.
  • Eligibility criteria are not defined in the sources and may not match MEL intent.
  • Combining insights per farmer complicates golden-dataset scoring.

Completion check. Every processed insight has either a generated survey or a logged reason code, and the step-level token and time logs are produced.

Claude’s proposed cuts. Use one model for both tiers if only one is available. Skip multi-insight grouping. Use deterministic pre-filters only if model calls are constrained.

Claude option A3

Deterministic send-window scheduler, simulated clock, broadcaster stub and tap app

Show timeliness and non-bombardment end to end: send windows from the crop calendar and action window, a per-farmer frequency cap, and delivery to a simulated app on a simulated clock.

Scope from the sources. Source fact: the scheduler is Must Have; the broadcaster and app are Should Have but appear in the Success Criteria. Scope: compute send_window deterministically; order surveys; apply the cap; advance the clock; deliver due surveys to a Streamlit or CLI tap view; record taps locally.

Minimum demo proposed by Claude. Advance the clock across part of the season and show surveys appearing only inside their windows. Show one farmer's second survey deferred by the cap, and record a tap answer in the simulated app.

AWS mapping, workflow, source support, and limits

AWS mapping

  • Amazon Bedrock: Upstream generation only; the scheduler itself is deterministic Mandatory for the agent per Technical.
  • Amazon RDS for PostgreSQL or Aurora PostgreSQL: Optional store for surveys, schedule, and responses Optional. The Technical document prefers it if a database is used. Confirm account availability. In-memory or local storage is acceptable for a simplified demo.

Inputs

  • Generated surveys (from OPT-1 or fixtures)
  • Crop calendar
  • Insight created_at and action_window_days
  • Configurable frequency cap

Implementation workflow

  1. Compute send_window per survey from the calendar and action window.
  2. Detect cap conflicts per farmer; defer or drop by rule.
  3. Advance the simulated clock or use a manual trigger.
  4. The broadcaster stub picks up due surveys.
  5. The simulated app renders single_choice items and records taps.

Source support

  • Vision: Features In Scope (MVP) (survey scheduler: Must Have; broadcaster stub and simulated app: Should Have)
  • Vision: MVP Success Criteria (scheduler orders by send window, per-farmer cap, broadcaster stub to simulated app)
  • Vision: MVP User Journeys (Journey 1: send window five to seven days after advice, weekly check)
  • Technical: Simulated Clock (shared clock; manual trigger for every step)
  • Technical: Reference Architecture (single process with a loop may stand in for the scheduler)
  • Technical: Infrastructure Preferences (deliver to the simulated app only)

Proposed extension. Proposed: send-window rules keyed on resolution_type and action_window_days, with a visible explanation per survey. Weather-dependent advice is labeled as an unresolved rule (source Open Question), not guessed. The cap value is a configurable parameter, not a fixed number.

Dependencies

  • Crop calendar source and format
  • Agreed cap rule
  • Survey fixtures if generation is not ready

Risks

  • Weather-dependent windows have no defined rule in the sources.
  • Cap conflict policy (defer, merge, or drop) is unspecified.
  • UI work can absorb time that evaluation needs.

Completion check. Every scheduled survey has a send_window that a deterministic check confirms; no farmer exceeds the configured cap; delivery happens only on the simulated clock to the simulated app.

Claude’s proposed cuts. Use a CLI instead of Streamlit, in-memory storage instead of RDS, a single-process loop, and a manual clock trigger only.

# FarmerChat Contextual MEL Survey Agent (MVP): Source Review

## Overall intent

Both documents describe a hackathon MVP. The MVP is a MEL (Monitoring, Evaluation and Learning) agent. It reads insights that FarmerChat already extracts from farmer conversations and turns them into short, personalized, tap-based follow-up surveys. Each survey is timed to the crop calendar and the advice's action window, then handed to a scheduler and broadcaster [Vision — Executive Summary].

The stated priority is survey quality, demonstrated through evaluation. Infrastructure and scale are secondary [Technical — Hackathon Approach and Expectations]. The work uses fully synthetic data only. There is no FarmerChat access, no Digital Green AWS access and no real farmer data [Technical — Hackathon Approach and Expectations; Vision — Business Constraints].

## Vision document

**Problem.** Today, evidence that farmers adopt the advice comes from field surveys and RCTs on small samples. These are expensive and cannot cover about 2.2M farmers. Earlier digital surveys were broadcast: every farmer got the same questions regardless of context, which yields little information and erodes trust [Vision — Problem Statement].

**Users** [Vision — Target Users and Stakeholders]:
- **Small-scale farmers in Bihar** need a few quick, relevant taps at a sensible time.
- **The MEL team** needs adoption and outcome data per recommendation.
- **Product and AI teams** need a clean contract into the broadcaster and app.
- **An external evaluation agency** needs a consistent, auditable collection method.

**Roadmap context.** The MEL Agent is the hackathon focus. NightSync, SeasonPlanner and FarmWatch are future phases [Vision — The Background Agent Vision].

**Design principles** [Vision — Design Principles]:
- One farmer, one context.
- Short and tappable.
- Ask at the right time.
- Quality before volume: no survey is better than an irrelevant one.
- Evaluate before scheduling.
- Contract over framework.
- Language passed as a BCP 47 parameter.

**Safety** [Vision — Safety Tenet]:
- Surveys must never collect personal information.
- Surveys must not give new advice or contradict the advice already given.

## Proposed end-to-end workflow

1. Ingest synthetic insights and query them by farmer, crop, topic and date [Vision — Features In Scope].
2. Decide whether an insight is eligible, then generate a survey per insight or group of insights in the requested language.
3. Self-evaluate (schema plus relevance). Failing surveys are not scheduled.
4. The scheduler assigns a send window from the crop calendar and action window, then applies a per-farmer frequency cap.
5. A broadcaster stub on the simulated clock delivers due surveys to a simulated app, which renders tap items and records answers.
6. The evaluation harness scores the outputs and the team iterates [Vision — MVP User Journeys].
